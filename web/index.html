Physics of a viral sculpture

It looks like it defies physics.
It's a pendulum in disguise.

A steel figure stands on one toe on a tiny plate, arms swept back, wobbling but never falling. The secret is two heavy weights hanging on long curved arms, which pull the centre of mass below the foot. Tip it, and gravity pulls it back.

03

The math, step by step

Estimated values read off the video: a 160 g figure and two steel weights of 240 g and 200 g. Pivot at the origin, y up.

PartMassxy

1 · Equilibrium: no net torque

Gravity acts at each mass. About the foot, the torque is the weight times the horizontal lever arm. For the upright position to be an equilibrium, the combined centre of mass must sit directly under the foot:

$$\sum \tau = -g\sum_i m_i x_i = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_{\text{cm}} = 0$$
$$160(0.5) + 240(-12) + 200(14) = 80 - 2880 + 2800 = 0\ \checkmark$$

That is why the arms can look lopsided: the heavier weight sits closer in, the lighter one farther out.

2 · Stability: where is the centre of mass vertically?

$$y_{\text{cm}} = \frac{\sum m_i y_i}{M} = \frac{160(8) - 240(28) - 200(16)}{600} = -14.4\ \text{cm}$$

The centre of mass is d = 14.4 cm below the foot. In general the sculpture is stable when the weights win:

$$m_1 L_1 + m_2 L_2 \;>\; m_f\, h_f \qquad (9920 > 1280)$$

3 · Potential energy and restoring torque

Tilt the whole rigid body by an angle θ. The centre of mass swings on a circle of radius d around the foot, so its height is \(-d\cos\theta\):

$$U(\theta) = -Mgd\cos\theta, \qquad \tau = -\frac{dU}{d\theta} = -Mgd\sin\theta$$

\(U\) is lowest at θ = 0. Any tilt raises the centre of mass, and the torque always points back to upright.

4 · Equation of motion: a physical pendulum

$$I\,\ddot\theta = -Mgd\sin\theta - c\,\dot\theta, \qquad I = \sum_i m_i\,(x_i^2 + y_i^2)$$

The damping term c stands for air drag and friction at the toe. Moment of inertia, part by part:

$$I = 160(64.25) + 240(928) + 200(452) \approx 3.23\times10^{5}\ \text{g·cm}^2 = 0.0323\ \text{kg·m}^2$$

5 · The period

For small angles, \(\sin\theta\approx\theta\) and the motion is simple harmonic:

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{Mgd}{I}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.848}{0.0323}} \approx 5.12\ \text{rad/s}, \qquad T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} \approx 1.23\ \text{s}$$

That's the same period as a simple pendulum \(L_{\text{eq}} = I/(Md) \approx 37\ \text{cm}\) long, even though the centre of mass is only 14 cm down. Weights far from the foot inflate I and give the slow, floaty sway.

6 · Remove the weights

The bare figure has its centre of mass 8 cm above the toe, so the sign flips and upright becomes a hilltop:

$$\ddot\theta = +\frac{g}{h}\,\theta \;\Rightarrow\; \theta(t) = \theta_0\cosh(\lambda t), \quad \lambda=\sqrt{g/h}\approx 11.1\ \text{s}^{-1}$$
$$1^\circ \to 30^\circ \text{ when } \cosh(\lambda t)=30 \;\Rightarrow\; t \approx 0.37\ \text{s}$$

Without the weights it would topple in about a third of a second. Try the No weights preset in the lab.
